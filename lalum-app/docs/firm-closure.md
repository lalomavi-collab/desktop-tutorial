# Closing a firm (offboarding a tenant)

Status: the checklist (0062), begin closing / cancel closing (0063), revoke / restore access (0067), finish closing (0065) and the export package (0066 and 0068, the ZIP builder and the paged signer for the originals) are built. Migration 0068 and version 2 of the signer are live (2026-10-21); 0069 removes the old unpaged function, and the originals step stays off until `VITE_EXPORT_ORIGINALS=1`. The other steps below are manual today; what is not built is listed at the end.

## What it means

A firm leaves the platform as a sequence: **stop activity, hand over the files, start the retention clock, revoke access and secrets, scrub after retention, keep a tombstone.** It is never a `DELETE` of the firm row. Verified on the live project on 2026-10-20:

- About 50 tables cascade from `lalum_firms`. A delete would drop matters, tax documents, trust ledger and signatures silently.
- The audit chain refuses row deletion (append only, by design), so a firm that has audit rows cannot be deleted. The firm row stays as a tombstone.
- Stored originals (250 today) are outside the foreign key graph and would be orphaned in Storage.
- Members' logins (`auth.users`) and secrets (`lalum_private.firm_secrets`, tax connections) are not removed by any cascade.

## Statuses (already in the schema)

`lalum_firms.status` is constrained to `ACTIVE | SUSPENDED | CANCELED`. Use them as: `ACTIVE` working, `SUSPENDED` closing (frozen, export allowed), `CANCELED` closed (dormant, then tombstone). Verified: the intake pipeline refuses any firm that is not `ACTIVE` (HTTP 402). **Not verified:** whether row level security or login behaves differently for `SUSPENDED` or `CANCELED`; the repository code uses the status only for display and for that pipeline check.

## Begin closing and cancel closing (0063)

`lalum_firm_begin_closing(firm, effective_date, reason, typed_firm_name, dry_run default true)`. Only the platform admin in a second factor session may call it: a partner asks, LALUM executes.

1. **Dry run first (the default).** It changes nothing and returns whether it would proceed, the blockers, how many matters would get a retention clock, how many of those are in the bin, whether the intake token would be cleared, and the manual steps.
2. **A real run** (`dry_run := false`) needs the firm's exact name typed, a reason of at least 10 characters, an effective date from today to one year ahead (a past date would shorten retention), and zero blockers from the checklist. It then sets the status to `SUSPENDED`, starts the retention clock where there is none (a matter that already has a date keeps it), clears the intake token, writes the closure record (`lalum_firm_closures`, with the reason and the ids it clocked) and `CLOSURE_STARTED` to the audit chain (ids, counts and fixed words only; the reason text is not in the chain).
3. **Read before a real run.** A matter in the bin also gets a retention clock, so it cannot be purged until the retention period after the effective date ends. The dry run counts them (`bin_matters_not_purgeable_until_retention_ends`).
4. **Not done by it, listed in the result as manual steps:** the tax connections and their vault secrets and the members' logins (both are `lalum_firm_revoke_access`, a separate step), the handover export.

`lalum_firm_cancel_closing(firm, reason)` takes it back: the previous status returns, the clocks it set are cleared (only those still equal to the effective date; one changed later is left alone), the closure record is marked cancelled, `CLOSURE_CANCELLED` goes to the chain. It refuses while a purge is in progress. It cannot restore the intake token: issue a new one.

**Do not run a real closing on the live firm.** It is the only tenant and the owner's own: suspending it stops the intake pipeline (HTTP 402), and its 14 bin matters would become unpurgeable for the retention period. A dry run is safe and is the way to look.

## Revoke access and restore access (0067)

`lalum_firm_revoke_access(firm, typed_firm_name, custodian default null, dry_run default true)`. The access half of the closure, run after begin closing: the firm is `SUSPENDED` with an active closure (`NO_ACTIVE_CLOSURE` otherwise). Same gate as 0063 and 0065: only the platform admin in a second factor session, a dry run by default, the firm's exact name typed. One chain row, `ACCESS_REVOKED`, with counts and fixed words only (no user id, no name).

- **Logins.** It bans the Auth login of every member except the custodian (an optional member who keeps read access for the handover) and deletes their sessions. The ban is `now() + 100 years`, never a delete, and the previous `banned_until` of each user is stored on the closure record, so restoring is exact. **It never touches a platform admin**, and never a user who is also a member of another firm that is not `CANCELED`: both are counted in the result (`logins_skipped_platform_admin`, `logins_skipped_other_firm`). Today `lalum_firm_members.user_id` is unique (one firm per login), so the second guard cannot trigger in production; it stays as a defence in case that ever changes. On the live firm the only member is also the platform admin, so even a mistaken real run would ban nobody.
- **A token already issued works until it expires.** Auth cannot recall a signed JWT. The ban stops the next token and the refresh; the result says `tokens_already_issued_work_until_they_expire`.
- **Tax connections.** It deletes the vault secrets (client secret and refresh token) of every tax connection of the firm, and the connection rows, so `lalum_firm_finish_closing` sees none left. **This is not reversible**: the firm must reconnect. It does not revoke the authorisation at the tax authority, which only the firm or the authority can do; that stays a listed manual step (`revoke_at_tax_authority`). The dry run says `tax_revocation_is_reversible: false`.
- **Restore.** `lalum_firm_restore_access(firm, reason)` (reason of at least 10 characters, same gate) puts back each stored `banned_until`, but only for a login that still carries the hundred year ban: one an admin has since changed by hand is left alone. It writes `ACCESS_RESTORED`. It cannot bring the tax connections back (`tax_connections_need_reconnecting` in the result).
- **Interplay.** `lalum_firm_cancel_closing` is replaced in 0067 and now refuses (`ACCESS_REVOKED`) while the logins are still banned: restore first, then cancel. A second revocation is refused (`ALREADY_REVOKED`) until a restore. The deletes run as dynamic SQL, the same habit as `lalum_finish_purge_matter`.
- **Do not run a real revocation on the live firm** (it would also end the sessions of anyone who is not the owner and the platform admin, and destroy tax credentials where there are any; there are none today). A dry run is safe and is the way to look.
- Test: `supabase/tests/firm_revoke_access.sql` (65 assertions), run after the stubs, 0018, 0014, 0019, 0059, 0060, 0061, 0062, 0063, 0065 and 0067. It creates stand ins for Auth sessions, `banned_until` and the vault, and drops the unique constraint on `user_id` inside its rolled back transaction to exercise the other firm guard.

## Finish closing (0065)

`lalum_firm_finish_closing(firm, reason, typed_firm_name, dry_run default true)`. Same gate as 0063: only the platform admin in a second factor session, a dry run by default, the firm's exact name typed and a reason of at least 10 characters for a real run.

- **It refuses (`BLOCKED`) while** any matter of the firm is not purged, a tax connection is left, an intake token is set, or the closing checklist reports a blocker. The dry run lists each one.
- **It reports, and does not gate,** `members_logins_not_banned` (members whose Auth login has no future `banned_until`). The platform does not own Auth and one custodian may stay, so banning is the manual step it names.
- **A real run** sets the status to `CANCELED`, stamps `finished_at`, `finished_by` and `finish_reason` on the closure record, and writes `CLOSURE_FINISHED` to the chain with counts and fixed words only (the reason text is not in the chain).
- **It cannot be undone.** `lalum_firm_cancel_closing` only works on `SUSPENDED`, and `lalum_firm_begin_closing` refuses a firm that is not `ACTIVE`. A firm that must come back is a new decision by the owner, not a function.
- Test: `supabase/tests/firm_finish_closing.sql`, run after the stubs, 0018, 0014, 0019, 0059, 0060, 0061, 0062, 0063 and 0065.

## Export package (0066)

The firm is owed its own records, complete and checkable. 0066 adds the read side; the ZIP is assembled in the browser (`src/lib/cockpit/firmExportCore.ts`, `firmExport.ts`, screen `src/pages/cockpit/FirmExport.tsx`). The Edge Function that signs the originals in one batch is **not built yet**, so the ZIP does not contain them.

- `lalum_firm_export_manifest(firm)`: per included table a row count and a sha256, the audit chain check (`lalum_verify_audit_chain`, first broken sequence or none, head sequence and hash), the originals in Storage (count, bytes, how many have no sha256), the excluded tables with a reason, and `unclassified`. Writes one `FIRM_EXPORT_MANIFEST` row to the chain (counts only), and writes it BEFORE counting (migration 0076), so the audit tables the package contains are counted with that row and the page totals agree with the manifest.
- `lalum_firm_export_page(firm, table, offset, limit)`: the rows of one table as the exact JSON text the hash was computed over, ordered by that text, at most 500 per call. Join all pages of a table with newlines and the sha256 equals the manifest value, so the receiver can verify each `.jsonl` file without trusting the platform.
- `lalum_firm_export_originals_page(firm, after, limit)` (0068): document, matter, path, sha256, size, mime and file name for the originals of live matters, at most 500 per call, keyset paging by document id; writes one `FIRM_EXPORT_ORIGINALS` row per page (count only). It replaced the unpaged `lalum_firm_export_originals(firm)` of 0066, which 0069 removes. Do not use `lalum_open_original` for 250 files: it writes one chain row and one 60 second link per file.
- **Who may call:** a partner or admin of the firm behind MFA with financial permission, or the platform admin in a second factor session. Allowed in any firm status, `CANCELED` included.
- **Classification, not silence.** Every table with a `firm_id` is included or excluded with a reason (blind index hashes, tax connection credentials, delivery queues, counters, platform records, client portal access, timers, import logs). A table added later and not decided shows in `unclassified` and `complete` turns false: do not hand over a package that says `complete: false`. Checked on the live project on 2026-10-20: 46 included, 11 excluded, none unclassified, none missing `firm_id`. A listed table that loses its `firm_id` fails the manifest loudly instead of exporting an empty file.
- **Names.** Documents are stored with party names replaced by tokens such as `[CLIENT_NAME_1]`; the real names are in the originals. A package without the originals is not a handover. The manifest reports their count so the receiver can check it.
- **Per matter export and the originals.** `ArchiveExport.tsx` used to tell the client the platform does not keep the original, which was false (250 live, 68 MB). The text is corrected, and when `VITE_OPEN_ORIGINAL` is `"1"` (the flag of the existing open original button, function `lalum-open-original` v3 is live) the ZIP now carries the originals too, in `originals/<NN>-<name>.<ext>` beside the text versions of the same document. Each original is opened through the same door as the cockpit button: partner or attorney with a second factor, ONE `DOCUMENT_ORIGINAL_OPENED` chain row per file (the hash of the stored file, no name), a 60 second link, fetched at once and checked against the size and sha256 recorded at intake (`original_size`, `original_sha256`, `original_mime`, read from the documents table; the path is never read by the browser). A retry reuses the same link, so one file is one chain row. Nothing is silent: a refused open, a failed fetch, a wrong size or hash, or no recorded hash is listed per document in `manifest.json` and in the README, and the screen says how many were not included. Above 150 files or 300 MB the originals are left out (`skipped: TOO_LARGE`) and the README says the firm hands them over separately; the live maximum is 83 documents in one matter. With the flag off the text says the original file is not included. A matter of 83 originals writes 83 chain rows plus the one export row, which is the point: the chain shows which originals left. Check: `npm run matter-originals-check` (10 assertions). Not run in a real browser yet.
- **The ZIP (client side).** A partner or admin of the firm opens `/workspace/export` and presses one button. The builder asks for the manifest, pulls every page (at most 500 rows each), rebuilds each `data/<table>.jsonl` and **checks its sha256 against the manifest before anything is zipped**. It refuses, and downloads nothing, when the manifest says `complete: false` or lists an unclassified table, when a table's row count changes during the export, or when any file does not hash to its recorded value. It adds `PACKAGE.json` (files, row counts, hashes, the audit chain result and head, the excluded tables with reasons, the originals summary, `handover_complete`) and a Hebrew `README.txt`. A broken audit chain is reported in the README and on the screen, not hidden and not a reason to refuse: it is a finding the firm must see. The file name is `firm-export-<first 8 characters of the firm id>-<date>.zip`, never a firm or client name.
- **Originals in the ZIP (behind a flag).** `supabase/functions/lalum-export-originals` (version 2) is called once per page of originals: it asks `lalum_firm_export_originals_page` (0068) as the signed in user (the database checks the right and writes **one** chain row per page, with the count only), then signs exactly the paths the database returned, in one batch, for 15 minutes, and returns document, matter, recorded sha256, size, mime, original file name, link, the cursor for the next page (`next_after`, null on the last) and the total. The request is `{firm_id, after, limit}`; the page is 100 by default and never above 200 (the database clamps at 500). Paging is keyset by document id, so a document added during the export cannot shift the pages. There is no ceiling on the firm's total any more (the 413 of version 1 is gone). The storage path never leaves the server and a path the caller names is never signed. The browser downloads a page completely before it asks for the next one (the links last 15 minutes, so signing everything first would let the early ones expire), skips an id it has already seen, stops if the cursor does not move or a page brings nothing new, and caps the walk by the count the manifest announced; a total that differs from the manifest during the walk ends with `list_error: COUNT_CHANGED`. The browser fetches four at a time (one retry), checks each file's size and sha256 against what the database recorded, and stores them as `originals/<document id>.<ext>` with `originals/index.jsonl` mapping each to its matter and original name: the archive paths never carry a file name, which can identify a client. **Nothing is silent:** a file that cannot be fetched, has the wrong size or hash, or has no recorded hash is listed in `PACKAGE.json` (`originals.failed`, by document and reason) and in the README, and `handover_complete` is false. A mismatched file is still included, because handing over what is stored while flagging it is more useful than omitting it. A failed or malformed list is not fatal: the verified tables are still delivered, with `list_error`. `handover_complete` is true only when the list matches the manifest count and every original is in the ZIP and verified.
- **Rollout.** The step is off until `VITE_EXPORT_ORIGINALS` is `"1"` (set after the function is deployed, as with `VITE_OPEN_ORIGINAL`), so merging this ships no step that fails. Off, the package states plainly that the originals are not included. Deploying the function needs the owner's approval.
- **Volumes (originals above 256 MB).** The browser no longer holds all the originals until one ZIP is written. The originals are cut into volumes of about 256 MB (`VOLUME_BYTES`; one file larger than that gets a volume of its own), each volume is zipped and handed to the browser as soon as it is cut, and the next file is not fetched until the previous volume has left memory, so memory stays near one volume plus four files in flight. A firm that fits in one volume still gets the single `firm-export-<id>-<date>.zip`. A larger one gets `-part-01.zip`, `-part-02.zip` and so on; the LAST part also carries the tables (`data/`), `PACKAGE.json`, `README.txt` and `originals/index.jsonl`, because only then is everything known (`PACKAGE.json` has `volumes`, and every index row has its `volume`). Every part has a `VOLUME-NN.json` with the path, size and recorded sha256 of each file in it, so one part can be checked alone; the README says to keep all parts together and that a package missing a part is not complete. The tables are still verified against the manifest hashes before the first ZIP is written; if a volume cannot be written the export stops with `VOLUME`, fetches nothing more, and the screen says to discard the partial files. The old 1 GiB refusal is gone; above 20 GiB (`MAX_ORIGINALS_BYTES`, about 80 parts) the originals are left out with `TOO_LARGE` and the firm hands them over separately. The browser pauses 1.5 seconds between downloads and may ask to allow several; the screen says so. Not built: writing straight to disk as one streamed ZIP (File System Access API, Chromium only, with a fallback still needed) or a server side package; volumes work in every browser. Run in a real browser on 2026-10-21 against a mock Supabase with three 90 MB originals: two parts downloaded, every original byte identical, `PACKAGE.json` in the last part only. Checks: `npm run firm-export-check` (36 assertions, including volumes, a memory ceiling, a boundary, an oversized file and a failing volume).

## Tax retention of accounting records (0070)

What was already protected: an issued tax document (`lalum_fin_documents`, 0014) and the Invoice4U mirror (`lalum_fin_archive`, 0015; 273 rows live, 2025-01-14 to 2026-10-07) cannot be deleted at all, by their own triggers, even after the period. That is stricter than the law and stays.

What was not: `lalum_fin_payments` and `lalum_fin_expenses` are accounting records too, and any user with financial permission could delete them the next day. 0070 closes that:

- `lalum_tax_retention_until(date)`: the last day a record of that date must be kept, 31 December of its year plus 7. The platform does not know the filing date, so it can enforce only the floor of "7 years from the end of the tax year, or 6 years from filing, whichever is later". A firm that filed late may owe longer; that stays the firm's to know. The floor is never shorter than the law.
- Delete of a payment or expense is refused (`TAX_RETENTION`) until that day has passed. The date and the firm of a kept row cannot be changed (`TAX_RETENTION_DATE_LOCKED`), which would move its clock. A row the platform took in during the last 24 hours can still be removed or re-dated, so that a mistaken entry can be undone. Amounts can still be corrected: a correction is not a deletion; auditing corrections is a separate question.
- Delete of a firm is refused (`TAX_RETENTION_FIRM`) while it holds an issued document, an archive row, or a payment or expense in retention, with a message that names the reason. Closing a firm (`CANCELED`) never deletes it, so the procedure is unaffected.
- The closure checklist (`lalum_firm_closure_checklist`, re-created in 0070) has one more INFO item, `accounting_records` (a count), and a top level `accounting_retained_until` (the latest such day, or null). Closing a firm does not remove these records; the checklist now says so.
- Live today: 273 archive rows, no issued documents, payments or expenses yet, so nothing is blocked retroactively and nothing is backfilled.
- Test: `supabase/tests/tax_retention.sql` (22 assertions, run after the stubs, 0018, 0014, 0015, 0019, 0059, 0060, 0061, 0062 and 0070): the rule on year edges and a leap day, refusal for the owner role and for a partner through the API role, date and firm locks, amount correction, the 24 hour window, a record past its period, firm delete with and without records, an archive row of any age, the checklist item and date.
- **Audit of corrections (0071).** Payments and expenses had no audit trigger at all, unlike the other billing tables. 0071 writes one row to the immutable billing audit log for every correction and every deletion: `FIN_RECORD_CORRECTED` lists, for amounts, dates, VAT figures, category, method and ids, the old and the new value; for free text (supplier, description, document reference, payment reference, notes) only the name of the field that changed, never its content, because those fields can hold a client or supplier name. An update that changes none of these (only the `updated_at` touch) writes nothing. `FIN_RECORD_DELETED` records the amounts, dates and ids. Both carry `within_undo_window`. Inserts are not audited (the row has `created_at` and `created_by`). A deletion refused by 0070 writes nothing. Test: `supabase/tests/fin_record_audit.sql` (23 assertions, after 0070 and 0071). Not built: a required reason for a correction after the undo window; it would change the screens and is a product decision.
- Owner decisions inside this: (1) keep the 24 hour undo window or remove it; (2) whether issued documents and the archive may ever be removed after the period (today never); (3) a privacy erasure request does not override this duty, and the contract with the firm should say so.

## Decisions that are the owner's

1. **Who holds the data after closure.** Recommended: the firm stays responsible for its client data and LALUM acts as processor; the contract must say what is returned, when it is deleted, and who is the custodian of the files. Not analysed here.
2. **Retention periods. Verified on 2026-10-20 against two independent sources each.**
   - *Attorney's client file:* Advocates Law s.90A(b), added by Amendment 42 (2024): **7 years** from the end of the attorney's handling or of the legal proceedings, unless the attorney and client agree otherwise in writing (s.90A(c)). Second Schedule item 3: documents of a real estate transaction or proceeding (in Israel or the Area) **25 years**, or another period agreed in writing. It covers "archival material" (any document that may serve as evidence), and **excludes internal documents** (drafts, internal notes) and anything another statute requires to be kept longer (s.90A(l)(2)). The 25 years attach to the document type, not to every document in a real estate matter. Sources: the consolidated statute text (Nevo) and the Knesset's published Amendment 42 text (Sefer HaChukim 3161) together with the Constitution Committee's press release. The old rules (5 years) are replaced; the amendment starts one year after publication, and the exact start date was not verified here.
   - *Accounting records:* Income Tax Instructions (Bookkeeping) 1973, instruction 25(c): **7 years from the end of the tax year, or 6 years from filing the return, whichever is later**; instruction 25(d): contracts, minutes and other documents kept voluntarily, **3 years at least** from filing. Sources: the consolidated text (Nevo) and the copy of the Instructions published by a law firm, matching a Deloitte guide and a CPA firm circular. Neither copy is the Official Gazette; check the Gazette before quoting a section number to a client.
   - The product's 7 and 25 match the law. The matter retention clock is a floor, not the whole duty: a firm that issued tax documents must also keep its accounting records for the tax period above. Migration 0070 enforces the floor of that period for accounting records (see the section below).
