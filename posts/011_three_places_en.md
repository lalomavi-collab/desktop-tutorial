Your legal exposure to AI is not in the model.

That is the most common mistake I meet. Executives audit the tool. Which model, where the data sits, is it encrypted. Good questions, all of them covering the one place where the damage almost never happens.

The damage happens in three much quieter places.

**One: the data that went in.**
Who put it there, where it came from, and whether there was permission to use it. A client contract pasted into a public model to "summarize this quickly" is not a shortcut. It is an incident.

**Two: the decision nobody documented.**
The system recommended, someone accepted, nobody recorded the basis. A year later, asked how the decision was reached, there is no answer. Missing documentation is not a technicality. In a legal proceeding it is often the whole case.

**Three: the vendor contract.**
Almost every AI licensing agreement moves responsibility for the output onto you. It is written there, quietly, in a clause nobody read. You signed, you own it.

All three look entirely sound on the day you sign.

All three detonate on the day someone starts asking questions.
