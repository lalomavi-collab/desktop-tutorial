An urban renewal agreement. One hundred and fifty pages.

Someone on the team did the entirely reasonable thing: ran it through a public language model and asked for a summary of the material clauses. Got back a clean, clear, convincing summary. Two minutes instead of two days.

The summary was correct. It simply was not complete.

One clause, a local authority carve-out, did not make it in. Not because the model erred. Because it was drafted in convoluted legal Hebrew, in a double negative, inside an annex, and the model read it as procedural.

It surfaced eight months later, at the planning committee.

By then there were signed residents, a schedule committed to the bank, and a budget built on different assumptions. The cost of that one clause was not only legal. It was cash flow.

What is interesting here is not that the AI missed it. A tired lawyer at one in the morning misses things too.

What is interesting is that nobody knew a check was required. There was no stage at which someone asked: this output, who approved it, and on what basis.
