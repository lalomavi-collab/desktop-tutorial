"We are an Israeli company, the EU AI Act does not apply to us."

I hear that sentence often, and in most cases it is not accurate.

The European regulation does not ask where the company is registered. It asks where the users are, and where the output of the system is used. If you have customers in Europe, or your product serves users there, the question is already on your desk.

And it is not one question. It is a chain:

Is your system classified as high risk?
Who is the provider and who is the deployer, legally rather than commercially?
Is there a documented risk management system?
Who signs the declaration of conformity?

Most organizations I meet cannot answer the first one, which is why they never reached the other three.

This is not an emergency. It is also not next year's problem. Classification is a few weeks of work when done in order, and considerably more when done under pressure from a customer demanding an answer or from a due diligence process.

The only difference between the two is when you start.

If you want to know where you stand on that chain, a diagnostic session is the right place to begin.
