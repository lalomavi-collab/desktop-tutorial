Over the past two weeks I have written here about the exposure that forms around AI: where it forms, what it looks like in a real case, and what the European regulation already requires.

Today I want to remove the last obstacle, because it is not information. It is not knowing what happens in the session.

So, exactly what happens:

**One hour. Not a sales call.**
I do not present services and I do not send a proposal at the end. If the conclusion after that hour is that you are in good shape, I say so.

**You talk for the first thirty minutes.**
What you are running, who touches it, and which decisions rest on the output. I ask questions rather than lecture.

**We map for the next thirty.**
The three places: the data, the decision, the vendor contract. Where you are exposed, how much, and what needs attention before what.

**You leave with three things.**
A written exposure map. A priority order for the next three months. And a clear view of what you can handle internally and what needs counsel.

Many of the people who sit across from me leave with a list they execute themselves. That is entirely fine. It is still a day on which the organization is less exposed than it was that morning.
