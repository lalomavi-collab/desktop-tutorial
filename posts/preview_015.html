טקסט הפוסט (אנגלית)

Insurers are putting AI into underwriting, pricing and claims. Each of those decisions touches whether a person gets covered and what they pay, so each one carries real legal exposure.

A few things worth getting right:

• Underwriting: a model trained on past data can copy patterns that have nothing to do with real risk, or lean on proxies for a trait you may not lawfully price on. Fairness testing, and a reason you can explain to the policyholder in plain words, are not optional.

• Pricing: precision is not the same as fairness. If a variable sets the premium, you should be able to show the business link between that variable and the insured risk, to a regulator and to a court.

• Claims: a model flag is a reason to review, never a reason to deny on its own. A human who can actually change the outcome has to stay in the loop.

And responsibility does not move to the vendor. If a third party builds the model, your contract needs audit rights, transparency on training data and versions, and limits on reuse of your customers' data.

AI in insurance is allowed. Delegating judgment is not. What cannot be explained and documented is not ready to run against customers.
